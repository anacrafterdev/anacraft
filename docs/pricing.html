Where the line is

Everything that reads your numbers is free, and stays free — the dashboard, the realtime feed, and every one-shot report. No account, no card, no expiry.

Four things do more than show you the numbers. craft configure creates the GA4 property for a domain and hands you its tag; craft audit checks how the property is measuring and tells you what is wrong with it; craft watch checks the numbers against their own recent normal and tells you what broke; craft mcp lets an assistant read the site for you. Those are the subscription, read as a ladder: Basic is what the original $2.99 plan was, Pro adds the audit and Slack delivery of a watch, and Elite adds the MCP server. Every plan includes the ones below it, and moving up is billed going forward, never charged again.

Starting one from the browser

Take the button above. Stripe asks for an email — use the one you sign in to Google with, because that is what hands the payment to your account afterwards. Then, on any machine:

# already signed in? craft subscribe --check does the same
craft login

Signing in is what claims the payment. There is no licence key, no code to paste, and nothing to keep anywhere — the account is the receipt. If you paid with a different email than the one on your Google account, send us both and we will move it across.

The rest of it

A second machine

# the same Google account is the whole of it
craft login
craft subscribe --check

--check only looks up where the account stands. It opens no browser, so it is also the one to run from a script or over SSH.

What the payment knows about you

One row: the Stripe customer, the subscription, its status, and the email the card was paid with — the key your Google account is matched against, once and one way. An unowned payment can be adopted by an account with the same email; an owned one can never be read by another. After that the account id is the only key, so changing your email later changes nothing.

Nothing about your analytics goes near it. The binary has no telemetry, and the subscription lookup does not know your site exists.

The gate is a line in your config

It is one supporter = true in a TOML file you own, in a binary you can rebuild from Apache-2.0 source. We are not going to pretend otherwise: no phone-home, no licence check on launch beyond the one keeping your own subscription current, and no telemetry anywhere in the binary.

So it is an honest ask rather than a lock — three plans, from the $2.99 that always bought the setup command and the watch, up to Elite for the ones that send you a Slack message or answer an assistant, from the people who wrote them.
